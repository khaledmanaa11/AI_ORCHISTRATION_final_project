  <action>
    `BeliefMap` over a `board_size × board_size` grid with five operations:

    - `observe_exact(cell)` — collapse to a delta. This is Regime A's entry point, called when
      the opponent's revealed action resolves to a known pre-turn cell.
    - `predict(state, rules)` — advance one joint turn through the motion model (Task 2).
    - `update(likelihood)` — multiply pointwise and renormalise.
    - `posterior()` / `argmax()` / `sample(rng)` — read accessors. `sample` takes an injected
      `random.Random` so 04-11's seeded selection is reproducible; it must not touch the module-
      level RNG and must not use `secrets` (that is reserved for nonces, Phase 6).

    Three invariants, asserted in tests and defended in code:
    1. every cell ≥ 0 and the grid sums to 1 within tolerance after every operation;
    2. an `update` whose likelihoods are all zero **returns the prior unchanged** — a hint or a
       scent reading that explains nothing must not zero the map;
    3. cells that are barriers or off-board carry zero mass permanently.

    Keep the grid representation dense and boring — 7×7 is 49 floats and clarity beats cleverness
    here. If this file approaches 150 lines, move the accessors to a sibling rather than compress.
  </action>
  <verify>
    Property tests over random grids: normalisation holds after arbitrary sequences of
    predict/update; an all-zero likelihood leaves the posterior identical; `sample` with a fixed
    seed is reproducible across runs and its empirical frequency over many draws matches the
    posterior within tolerance; barrier cells never gain mass.
  </verify>
  <done>A well-behaved distribution that cannot silently become invalid.</done>

  <action>
    `spread(prior, role, state, rules) -> grid` — for each cell with mass, enumerate that role's
    **legal** actions from `sdk/actions.py` with the opponent hypothesised at that cell, and
    distribute the mass across the resulting cells.

    The default action prior is uniform over the legal set. That is deliberately weak and
    deliberately honest: we do not yet model *how* the opponent chooses. Leave a documented seam
    — an optional per-action weighting argument — so a future phase can drop in an opponent model
    without reshaping the call site. Do not invent a weighting now; an unjustified prior is worse
    than a uniform one.

    A cop's barrier action leaves the cop's cell unchanged but changes the board; the motion
    model only tracks position, so a barrier action contributes to the STAY branch. Say so in a
    comment — it is the kind of thing that looks like a bug six weeks later.
  </action>
  <verify>
    A delta prior on an interior cell spreads to exactly the 5 reachable cells with equal mass.
    A cell walled on three sides spreads only to the legal remainder. Mass is conserved to
    within float tolerance for every prior tested. The thief's spread never exceeds 5 cells and
    the cop's positional spread never exceeds 5 (its extra actions are barriers, not moves).
  </verify>
  <done>Uncertainty grows the way the rules allow, and no faster.</done>
</task>

  <action>
    `scent_likelihood(opponent_field, model, config) -> grid`. The chain D-42 specifies:

    1. **Invert the law.** A measured strength `τ` at a cell implies an age — the number of joint
       turns since the opponent deposited there — obtained from `expected_strength_after`. Cap
       the age at a configured maximum; below the configured epsilon a cell carries no
       information and gets the neutral likelihood, not zero (invariant 2 above).
    2. **Age → freshness weight.** Fresher implies more likely to be near. The mapping is a
       configured monotone weight; it is an engineering default and is labelled one.
    3. **Project to NOW.** Run the age-weighted field forward through `spread` for as many turns
       as the implied age, so the likelihood describes where the opponent *is*, not where it
       *was*. **This step is what stops us chasing the strongest cell** — the failure mode D-42
       names explicitly.

    Add the belief group to `language.json`: scent weight, epsilon, age cap, freshness mapping.
    Every one is an engineering default and the comment says so.
  </action>
  <verify>
    The decisive test: a trail deposited at cell A and then decayed for `k` turns, with the
    opponent actually having walked away from A, produces a posterior whose argmax is **not** A.
    Also: a fresh trail (age 0) concentrates near its source; a trail below epsilon everywhere
    leaves the prior unchanged; the recovered age of a synthetic field built by `k` applications
    of `decay` equals `k` within tolerance.
  </verify>
  <done>Scent moves the belief toward where the opponent went, not where it has been.</done>
</task>
