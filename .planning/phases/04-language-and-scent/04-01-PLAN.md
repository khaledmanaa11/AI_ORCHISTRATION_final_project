  <action>
    Two functions over a `ScentModel` and a board size, both pure and free of I/O:

    `emission(model, centre, board_size) -> dict[Coord, float]` — the Δτ contribution of an
    agent standing at `centre`. Overlay the kernel centred on that cell, **clipped at the board
    edge**: cells outside the board are dropped, never wrapped and never folded back.

    `decay(field, model) -> dict[Coord, float]` — apply
    `τ(t+1) = max(0, (1−ρ)·τ(t) + Δτ)` with `Δτ = 0` (emission is added separately by the
    caller, so the two operations compose without either one knowing about the other). Drop
    cells whose value falls below a configured epsilon so the grid does not accumulate a long
    tail of near-zero floats over 35 turns.

    Also expose `expected_strength_after(model, turns) -> float` — the strength a source cell
    retains after `n` turns of decay with no re-emission. This is the inverse the belief map's
    scent likelihood needs (D-42) and the quantity §4.4's lie-detection example computes; put it
    here so exactly one module owns the law.
  </action>
  <verify>
    Unit tests assert: the centre cell of `emission` equals `source`; the kernel is reproduced
    exactly at every offset; a centre at `(0,0)` writes only the in-board quadrant and never a
    negative index; `decay` applied once to `{c: 0.9}` yields `0.81` (the worked example, from
    the config, not typed into the test); ten applications of `decay` match
    `expected_strength_after(model, 10)` to within float tolerance; `decay` never returns a
    negative value even for an adversarially negative input.

  <done>Emission and decay are the book's, provably, and both are edge-safe.</done>

  <action>
    `ScentField` holds **two** independent grids: `own` (the trail we emit) and `opponent` (our
    local reconstruction of theirs, D-49). It exposes:

    - `emit_own(cell)` / `emit_opponent(cell)` — add an emission at a cell;
    - `advance()` — apply one joint-turn decay to **both** grids. Called exactly once per joint
      turn, after both actions are known. `RULES-RESOLUTION.md` §4 fixes one joint turn = one
      increment; the field must not be advanced per half-turn;
    - `strength(grid, cell)` and `freshest(grid)` — read accessors;
    - `snapshot()` — an immutable copy for the belief map and, later, the replay log.

    The opponent grid is fed by whatever the caller can legitimately establish about their
    movement — in Regime A their revealed cell each turn, in Regime B an emission weighted by
    the belief posterior. **This module does not decide which regime applies**; it takes cells
    (or cell→weight pairs) and applies the model. Keeping that decision out of here is what lets
    plan 04-05 own it without a circular import.

    Two grids on one object is not shared state: both belong to a single peer's own process and
    describe that peer's own beliefs. Nothing here is importable across the two processes.
  </action>
  <verify>
    Tests assert: `advance()` decays both grids by exactly one step; emitting on `own` never
    perturbs `opponent`; `snapshot()` is not aliased to the live grid (mutating the returned
    object leaves the field unchanged); a field advanced 35 times from a single deposit has all
    values ≥ 0 and matches `expected_strength_after`.
  </verify>
  <done>A peer can maintain both trails for a full-length game without drift or aliasing.</done>
</task>
