    the audit can prove the flag was honest about itself.

    `ClaimKind` enumerates what a hint can be about, and **marks which kinds are always true**:
    a capture declaration (rule 21/22) and a barrier declaration (rule 15/16). `plan_deception`
    refuses to construct a `LIE` plan for those kinds — it raises, loudly, because reaching that
    branch means a caller has a logic error that would disqualify the team.

    build the plan through one function and let the type make the illegal state unrepresentable.

    `plan_deception(role, state, belief, rng, config)` dispatches to the role policy, applies the
    always-true rule, and returns the plan. It never returns `None` — a turn always carries a
    hint (LANG-01), so a policy that cannot think of a good lie returns a truthful claim.
  </action>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: the thief — danger-adaptive lying (D-37)</name>
  <files>src/pursuit/strategy/deception_thief.py, config/police/deception.json, config/thief/deception.json, src/pursuit/shared/deception_config.py</files>
  <read_first>
    src/pursuit/strategy/belief.py (posterior and argmax — the cop-distance estimate's source)
    src/pursuit/strategy/features.py (existing distance helpers — reuse, do not re-derive)
  </read_first>
  <action>
    Estimate the cop's distance from our belief posterior — an expected Manhattan distance over
    the posterior, not the distance to its argmax, because a bimodal belief with a close mode is
    exactly the situation where the argmax lies to us.

    Lie probability rises as that distance falls, through configured thresholds. Two properties
    that matter more than the curve's exact shape:

    - **A truth floor.** Even at maximum danger, some turns tell the truth. D-37 calls this
      sprinkling truths so the lies stay credible; mechanically it stops the opponent's
      reliability coefficient from collapsing to its lower bound, after which our channel is
      worthless. The floor is configured and non-zero.
    - **The lie must be survivable under a scent check.** Prefer a claimed region that our own
      trail does not immediately contradict — §4.4's worked example is the opponent running
      exactly that test on us. Score candidate claims by how far they sit from our freshest
      trail cells and pick among the plausible ones.

    When truthful, the claim is a *true but unhelpful* statement where possible — truth is a
    requirement on the flag, not a duty to be maximally informative.
  </action>
  <verify>
    Over many seeded draws: lie frequency increases monotonically as the estimated cop distance
    falls; it never reaches 1.0 (the truth floor holds); it never reaches 0.0 at long range if
    configured non-zero. A claim chosen while a fresh trail sits north is not a claim of being
    north. Identical seeds reproduce identical sequences.
  </verify>
  <done>The thief lies when it matters, tells enough truth to stay believed, and does not contradict its own scent.</done>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: the cop — herding lies (D-38)</name>
  <files>src/pursuit/strategy/deception_cop.py</files>
  <read_first>
    src/pursuit/strategy/graph/territory.py and components.py (the cop's control structures from Phase 3)
    src/pursuit/strategy/belief.py (where we think the thief is)
  </read_first>
  <action>
    The cop's objective is different in kind: it is not hiding, it is **driving movement**. Pick
    the claim that, if believed, makes the thief step toward a cell the cop controls — a barrier
    cluster, a low-connectivity region, a corner. Phase 3 already computes territory and
    component structure; use those, do not invent a second notion of control.

    Concretely: for each candidate claim, ask what a believing thief would do — a thief that
    trusts *"the cop is over by the river"* moves away from the river. Score by the value of the
    cell it would move toward, using the existing evaluation rather than a new heuristic. Choose
    the best-scoring claim; if none beats the truthful claim, tell the truth.

    Keep the lookahead to one step. A deeper model of the thief's belief update is a research
    project, and its assumptions would be unverifiable in a league game where we never see the
    opponent's internals.
  </action>
  <verify>
    On a constructed board where one region is walled and another is open, the chosen lie is the
    one that would push a believing thief toward the walled region. When no claim improves on the
    truth, the plan is truthful. The policy never proposes a barrier-related lie (Task 1's gate
    is not the only defence; this policy does not generate the kind at all).
  </verify>
  <done>The cop's words do work the cop's legs cannot do in one turn.</done>
