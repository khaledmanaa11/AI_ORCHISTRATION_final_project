<objective>
Turn a `DeceptionPlan` into at most fifteen English words that are legal to send, and be
incapable of returning anything else.

Three ways this can go wrong in a league game — an over-length hint, a hint containing
coordinates, and no hint at all — are all protocol violations. This plan closes all three by
construction rather than by care.
</objective>

  <action>
    `bluff_prompt.py` holds the system prompt and the style guide (D-39). The guide, in short:
    concrete over vague; consistent with the board so a scent check does not immediately falsify
    it; the arena's real places when the arena is set; no meta-commentary, no hedging, no
    coordinates; **at most the configured number of words**, interpolated from config as §6.5.1
    requires. The prompt receives the claim and the intent as data and is told to phrase, not to
    decide.

    `compose(plan, context) -> str`:
    1. If the budget is `TEMPLATE_ONLY`, or the provider is `template`, or there is no key — go
       straight to the bank. No call, no failure, no latency.
    2. Otherwise call the provider. On any `LlmFailure` → bank.
    3. Count the result. Over the limit → **one** retry with an explicit shortening instruction.
       Still over → truncate.
    4. Validate with `assert_no_coordinates`. On violation → bank (do not attempt to repair text
       the model produced; a rewrite risks a second violation and costs another call).
    5. Return.

    `compose` has no failure mode. Every branch ends in a legal string. Assert that in the
    docstring and prove it in the tests.
  </action>

    mock completions asserts the result is always non-empty, always in-limit, always
    coordinate-free — and that `compose` never raises.
  </verify>
