  <files>src/pursuit/services/language_turn.py</files>
  <read_first>
    src/pursuit/services/llm/decode.py and bluff.py (the two calls to sequence)
    src/pursuit/strategy/deception.py (plan_deception)
    src/pursuit/network/deadline.py and watchdog.py (the turn's time budget)
  </read_first>
  <action>
    A single async function per turn with two halves:

    `decode_incoming(hint_envelope) -> Inference` — awaited under the configured response timeout;
    a timeout yields `NO_EVIDENCE` and the turn proceeds. This is the only place the timeout
    policy for decode lives.

    `compose_outgoing(plan, context) -> str` — same treatment; a timeout falls back to the bank.

    Putting both in one module means the timeout-and-fallback policy is stated once. Scattering
    it across the call sites is how one path ends up without it, and that path is the one that
    forfeits a game.

    **Time budget:** the turn has `network.response_timeout` and the watchdog has
    `watchdog_threshold`. The two language calls plus the decision must fit inside the smaller.
    Compute the per-call budget from config rather than hardcoding a split, and log when a call
    is skipped because the remaining budget was too small — an honest skip beats a forfeit.
  </action>
  <verify>
    A provider that never returns is abandoned at the configured deadline and the turn completes.
    Both halves fall back correctly on timeout. The remaining-budget skip triggers when the
    decision has already consumed most of the turn.
  </verify>
  <done>The language layer can be slow, but it can never stop a turn.</done>
</task>
