<objective>
Deliver the non-learned half of the decision engine: the Bayes motion-model prior, the fallback
policy that STRAT-02 requires for states the Q-table has never visited, and `HeuristicBrain` —
a fully playable brain that is simultaneously the fallback's home and the objective baseline
that `QLearningBrain` must beat in GATE-4.

Two things make this plan load-bearing beyond STRAT-02. First, without a *playable* baseline
the phase's central claim — "the RL learned something" — is unfalsifiable; a stub baseline can
be beaten by anything. Second, this is where the Phase-4 seam is set: `spread()` is written as
a prediction step with no evidence term precisely so that Phase 4 plugs scent and hint evidence
into the same update instead of rewriting it (D-10, D-11).

Output: `prior.py`, `fallback.py`, `heuristic.py` and their tests.
</objective>

      uniform(cells) -> dict[cell, float]
      spread(prior, state, agent, params) -> dict[cell, float]
      argmax_cell(prior) -> cell

    `spread` is the **prediction step**: each cell's mass is distributed evenly over the moves
    the opponent could legally make from it (via the engine's `legal_moves`, so barriers and
    bounds are honoured for free). No evidence term, no likelihood multiply — Phase 4 adds
    those here.

    Write a module docstring stating that explicitly, so a Phase-4 reader knows this is the
    prediction half of a Bayes update and not a finished belief filter.

    Normalize after every spread and assert the invariant in the function, not only in tests —
    a prior that quietly stops summing to 1 degrades the fallback silently.

    `argmax_cell` must break ties deterministically (documented rule), so replays reproduce.
  </action>

    Tests first. Then `fallback.py` exposing a `pick(obs, state, agent, params, prior=None)`
    that returns a `Decision` with `source=MoveSource.FALLBACK`.

    Behaviour, for the cop (pursue) and the thief (evade) respectively:
    - **Cop:** step that minimizes BFS distance to the believed target cell.
    - **Thief:** step that maximizes BFS distance from the believed cop cell, breaking ties
      toward the step with more onward legal moves — walking into a pocket to gain one square
      of distance is how an evader loses.
    - Where a `prior` is supplied, the believed target is its `argmax_cell`; where the caller
      supplies `obs.target_cell` directly (Phase-3 known-target case), use that. Both paths
      exist because D-11 fixes the *contract* at "a believed cell" while Phase 3 supplies a
      known one.

    Unreachable target: fall back to a legal move chosen by the documented deterministic rule
    rather than raising — the cop may legally wall the thief off.

    Never compute distance any way other than through `bfs()` (D-09, QUAL-02).
  </action>
